## Operational handbook: applied scenarios and decision guidance

The following illustrative procedures explain how to apply the policy rules above. They are fictional examples, not additional entitlements, deadlines or exceptions. Where a scenario is ambiguous, the controlling rule remains the policy statement above and the responsible owner must clarify it.

### Manager approval threshold

**Rule and interpretation.** Purchases above 500 USD require manager approval before an order is placed. The rule uses above, so the stated threshold is not automatically triggered by a purchase of exactly 500 USD.

**Worked scenario.** A team member considering a 620 USD software subscription must obtain manager approval before committing to the purchase.

**Employee workflow.** Prepare a business justification, total anticipated cost and supplier details; obtain and retain approval before ordering.

**Owner responsibilities.** Managers review budget and need; procurement advises on additional thresholds.

**Scope and limits.** The policy does not authorize bypassing other applicable controls for purchases at or below 500 USD.

### Competitive quotes

**Rule and interpretation.** Purchases above 5,000 USD require at least three comparable vendor quotations unless procurement approves an exception. Comparability matters as much as the count.

**Worked scenario.** Three quotes for materially different products may not allow a fair comparison even if the purchasing file contains three documents.

**Employee workflow.** Define requirements consistently; request comparable scope and pricing; document the comparison and any procurement-approved exception.

**Owner responsibilities.** Procurement validates the competition process and records exceptions.

**Scope and limits.** The rule does not state that the lowest price must always win or establish a sole-source approval formula.

### Vendor onboarding and due diligence

**Rule and interpretation.** New suppliers must complete vendor due diligence before payment. The existence of an invoice does not replace the onboarding requirement.

**Worked scenario.** A first-time consultant may finish an engagement, but finance should not release payment before the required supplier checks are complete.

**Employee workflow.** Submit vendor information through approved channels; verify documentation is complete; avoid exchanging sensitive supplier data in informal messages.

**Owner responsibilities.** Procurement and finance coordinate the supplier checks and payment readiness.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not enumerate all due-diligence documents or promise an onboarding completion time.

### Purchase orders

**Rule and interpretation.** Purchases above 1,000 USD require an approved purchase order. This is distinct from the manager-approval threshold and the three-quote threshold.

**Worked scenario.** A 1,500 USD equipment order exceeds both the 500 USD manager-approval threshold and the 1,000 USD purchase-order threshold, but not the 5,000 USD competition threshold.

**Employee workflow.** Check each applicable threshold independently; obtain approvals; ensure the purchase order exists before proceeding with the order.

**Owner responsibilities.** Procurement confirms purchase-order details and finance matches supporting documentation.

**Scope and limits.** This explanation does not define tax treatment or allow retrospective purchase orders as routine practice.

### Prohibition on splitting

**Rule and interpretation.** Splitting purchases to bypass approval thresholds is prohibited. Related transactions should not be artificially divided to avoid controls.

**Worked scenario.** Dividing a 6,000 USD requirement into two 3,000 USD orders solely to avoid three comparable quotations conflicts with the rule.

**Employee workflow.** Describe the complete business requirement and expected total; disclose related purchases; ask procurement about a legitimate phased requirement.

**Owner responsibilities.** Procurement evaluates the full purchasing context and records justified exceptions.

**Scope and limits.** Separate genuine business needs are not automatically improper; the prohibited purpose is evasion of approval thresholds.


## Reference workflow and recordkeeping

For any question about this policy, first locate the applicable rule and identify its exact trigger: a calendar-day deadline, a working-day deadline, an approval threshold, a role requirement, or an information-classification restriction. Next, check the relevant record in the approved system and obtain written approval wherever the rule requires it. Keep only the records necessary to show compliance, and share them only with authorized personnel. If the written policy does not address a situation, ask the designated owner rather than extrapolating from an example.

When communicating a decision, distinguish the policy requirement from a manager’s operational choice. Note the policy heading, relevant dates, evidence consulted, approver and any unresolved question. Escalate disputes to the named function where one is specified. The examples in this handbook are educational and do not supersede contracts, applicable law, or approved company policy updates.
