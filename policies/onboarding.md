## Operational handbook: applied scenarios and decision guidance

The following illustrative procedures explain how to apply the policy rules above. They are fictional examples, not additional entitlements, deadlines or exceptions. Where a scenario is ambiguous, the controlling rule remains the policy statement above and the responsible owner must clarify it.

### First-day identity and payroll tasks

**Rule and interpretation.** New employees complete identity verification and payroll forms on their first working day. This is a first-day obligation, not a task to postpone indefinitely.

**Worked scenario.** A new starter whose first day is Monday should be prepared to complete the required verification and payroll paperwork that day.

**Employee workflow.** Follow HR instructions for secure submission; check that personal details are accurate; confirm receipt of the required forms.

**Owner responsibilities.** HR coordinates verification and payroll setup and directs the employee to approved submission channels.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not specify acceptable identity documents or local payroll law.

### Security awareness deadline

**Rule and interpretation.** Security awareness training must be completed within the first seven calendar days. Calendar days include weekends.

**Worked scenario.** A new employee starting near the weekend should not assume the training deadline is seven working days later.

**Employee workflow.** Locate the assigned training promptly; complete the required module; retain or confirm the completion record.

**Owner responsibilities.** Managers allow time for training and HR or security tracks completion.

**Scope and limits.** This text does not change the seven-calendar-day requirement or create an automatic extension.

### Equipment and account readiness

**Rule and interpretation.** New starters should receive and use authorized work equipment and access consistent with the information-security policy. Account setup does not permit password sharing.

**Worked scenario.** A colleague may offer their login when a new hire lacks access, but shared credentials would conflict with the password-sharing prohibition.

**Employee workflow.** Report missing equipment or access to the responsible support team; use only assigned credentials; enable required MFA.

**Owner responsibilities.** IT resolves provisioning issues and the manager adjusts assignments while access is pending.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not promise a particular device model or a fixed hardware delivery date.

### Policy orientation scheduling

**Rule and interpretation.** HR schedules a policy orientation during the first two weeks of employment. Orientation is separate from the earlier seven-calendar-day security-training requirement.

**Worked scenario.** A new hire might attend HR orientation in week two but still needs to complete security training within the first seven calendar days.

**Employee workflow.** Attend the scheduled orientation; record questions about benefits, conduct and information security; consult the actual written policies afterward.

**Owner responsibilities.** HR arranges the session and explains where current policies can be found.

**Scope and limits.** Orientation attendance does not replace written approval required under other policies.

### Probation and contract exceptions

**Rule and interpretation.** The standard probationary period is 90 calendar days unless a contract or local law specifies otherwise. The exception is explicit and should be checked before stating a final date.

**Worked scenario.** An employee whose employment agreement specifies a different lawful probation period should not automatically assume the 90-day default overrides the contract.

**Employee workflow.** Review the employment agreement and HR record; ask HR to resolve inconsistencies; keep a copy of formal probation communications.

**Owner responsibilities.** HR interprets the applicable contract and law, while managers document performance feedback through approved processes.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not create an automatic termination rule or guarantee of confirmation at day 90.


## Reference workflow and recordkeeping

For any question about this policy, first locate the applicable rule and identify its exact trigger: a calendar-day deadline, a working-day deadline, an approval threshold, a role requirement, or an information-classification restriction. Next, check the relevant record in the approved system and obtain written approval wherever the rule requires it. Keep only the records necessary to show compliance, and share them only with authorized personnel. If the written policy does not address a situation, ask the designated owner rather than extrapolating from an example.

When communicating a decision, distinguish the policy requirement from a manager’s operational choice. Note the policy heading, relevant dates, evidence consulted, approver and any unresolved question. Escalate disputes to the named function where one is specified. The examples in this handbook are educational and do not supersede contracts, applicable law, or approved company policy updates.
