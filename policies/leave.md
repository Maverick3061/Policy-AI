# Annual Leave and Time Off

Fictional Acme Northstar Ltd. internal policy. Version 1.0. Effective 2026-01-01.

## Eligibility

Full-time employees receive 20 working days of paid annual leave per calendar year, prorated for partial years.

### Procedure and accountability for eligibility

## Operational handbook: applied scenarios and decision guidance

The following illustrative procedures explain how to apply the policy rules above. They are fictional examples, not additional entitlements, deadlines or exceptions. Where a scenario is ambiguous, the controlling rule remains the policy statement above and the responsible owner must clarify it.

### Annual leave accrual and partial-year service

**Rule and interpretation.** The 20-working-day annual entitlement is a full-calendar-year figure. Proration applies when service covers only part of that year; the HR balance is the authoritative operational record, not an informal spreadsheet.

**Worked scenario.** An employee joining midway through a year should consult the HR portal before requesting an extended absence. The published entitlement should not be interpreted as an automatic 20-day balance for a partial year.

**Employee workflow.** Check the recorded start date and current annual leave balance; compare the proposed absence with the balance shown; send a discrepancy to HR before relying on the figure. Record any correction in the HR system.

**Owner responsibilities.** Managers review requests against the recorded balance and team coverage. HR resolves disputed balances; managers should not independently create extra days.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not establish a new accrual formula, payout entitlement, or probation restriction.

### Planning and approval timeline

**Rule and interpretation.** The policy requires annual leave requests at least 10 calendar days before the proposed start and states managers normally respond within three working days. The two intervals use different day-counting conventions.

**Worked scenario.** If leave starts on a Monday, the employee should count back calendar days, including weekends, to decide when to submit. A manager receiving the request on Friday measures the normal response window in working days.

**Employee workflow.** Submit through the HR portal; include start and end dates, a coverage note and a reachable contact if appropriate; wait for approval before making irreversible commitments.

**Owner responsibilities.** The manager considers operational coverage and replies through the recorded workflow. If the request cannot be approved, the employee should discuss alternative dates.

**Scope and limits.** Submitting on time does not guarantee approval, and this text does not create an emergency-leave exception.

### Sick leave and evidence

**Rule and interpretation.** The policy provides 10 paid sick days per calendar year. A medical certificate is required when the absence is longer than three consecutive working days.

**Worked scenario.** An absence covering four consecutive working days requires a certificate. Three consecutive working days do not meet the stated longer-than-three trigger, although other lawful requirements may apply.

**Employee workflow.** Notify the normal absence contact promptly; enter the absence into the designated HR workflow; provide the certificate when the stated threshold is met; avoid disclosing more medical detail than needed.

**Owner responsibilities.** HR checks the leave record and required documentation. Managers should focus on scheduling coverage rather than requesting unnecessary medical history.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not define sick leave carryover, medical privacy law, or paid leave beyond the ten-day rule.

### Year-end balances and carryover

**Rule and interpretation.** A maximum of five unused annual leave days may be carried into the next year, and carried days must be used by March 31.

**Worked scenario.** If a worker ends December with seven unused days, this policy authorizes carrying at most five; it does not promise that the other two will be paid out.

**Employee workflow.** Review balances before year-end; plan usage early in the next year; identify any carried days in the portal; ask HR about discrepancies before March 31.

**Owner responsibilities.** HR maintains balances and managers assess leave coverage. Neither party should imply an exception without policy-owner written approval.

**Scope and limits.** The policy does not state a cash-out rule or how carried days are consumed relative to new-year days.

### Disputes and handover planning

**Rule and interpretation.** Manager approval and operational coverage remain conditions for annual leave. HR is the stated escalation point for disputed balances.

**Worked scenario.** Two team members may request overlapping dates. Both may have sufficient balances, but a manager can still assess whether the team can safely maintain operations.

**Employee workflow.** Provide a concise handover, current task status and expected return date. If a balance looks incorrect, document the issue for HR separately from the scheduling decision.

**Owner responsibilities.** Managers document coverage decisions consistently. HR resolves balance questions; the policy owner considers written exceptions where lawful.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not create a first-come-first-served priority rule or a guaranteed appeal deadline.
