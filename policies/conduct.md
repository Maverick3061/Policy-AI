## Operational handbook: applied scenarios and decision guidance

The following illustrative procedures explain how to apply the policy rules above. They are fictional examples, not additional entitlements, deadlines or exceptions. Where a scenario is ambiguous, the controlling rule remains the policy statement above and the responsible owner must clarify it.

### Respectful workplace behavior

**Rule and interpretation.** Employees must follow the respectful-conduct policy in meetings, messages and interactions with colleagues, customers and suppliers.

**Worked scenario.** Disagreement about a project decision can be expressed professionally without personal insults, intimidation or retaliation.

**Employee workflow.** Address the work issue directly, keep relevant written records when necessary and seek help from HR if conduct becomes concerning.

**Owner responsibilities.** Managers respond to concerns consistently and avoid dismissing reports merely because the parties disagree.

**Scope and limits.** This guidance does not replace formal investigation procedures or define disciplinary sanctions.

### Conflicts of interest

**Rule and interpretation.** Potential conflicts should be disclosed through the organization’s compliance process rather than privately managed by the affected employee.

**Worked scenario.** An employee participating in a supplier decision should disclose a close personal relationship with a bidder before taking part in evaluation.

**Employee workflow.** Describe the relationship or interest accurately; notify the designated compliance contact; follow any resulting instructions about participation.

**Owner responsibilities.** Compliance determines appropriate management of the conflict and documents the decision.

**Scope and limits.** A disclosure is not automatically a finding of misconduct, nor does it guarantee permission to proceed.

### Gifts from vendors

**Rule and interpretation.** Vendor gifts worth more than 50 USD must be declined or disclosed to compliance. The wording is more than 50 USD, not 50 USD or more.

**Worked scenario.** A gift valued at 75 USD falls above the threshold and must be declined or disclosed; a gift exactly at 50 USD does not trigger this particular greater-than threshold.

**Employee workflow.** Estimate value in good faith; document the giver and business context; contact compliance before retaining a gift that exceeds the threshold.

**Owner responsibilities.** Compliance evaluates disclosures and potential influence on business decisions.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not imply that gifts below the threshold are always appropriate or permitted in every circumstance.

### Reporting suspected misconduct

**Rule and interpretation.** Employees may report misconduct to the ethics mailbox or HR, and retaliation for good-faith reports is prohibited.

**Worked scenario.** A staff member who observes possible misconduct may contact HR even without resolving every factual uncertainty in advance.

**Employee workflow.** Describe observed events rather than speculation; preserve relevant records; use an approved reporting channel; avoid unnecessary circulation of allegations.

**Owner responsibilities.** HR or the ethics function assesses the report and maintains appropriate confidentiality.

**Scope and limits.** The policy does not promise anonymous reporting, a fixed investigation duration or a particular outcome.

### Continuing confidentiality duties

**Rule and interpretation.** Employees must protect company and customer confidential information even after employment ends.

**Worked scenario.** A former employee preparing a portfolio must not include confidential customer data simply because they no longer work for the company.

**Employee workflow.** Use only information authorized for release; return or protect confidential records according to the applicable process; ask before publishing uncertain material.

**Owner responsibilities.** Managers and information owners clarify permissible disclosure while the employee remains engaged.

**Scope and limits.** This section does not create a new non-compete obligation or prohibit use of general professional skills.


## Reference workflow and recordkeeping

For any question about this policy, first locate the applicable rule and identify its exact trigger: a calendar-day deadline, a working-day deadline, an approval threshold, a role requirement, or an information-classification restriction. Next, check the relevant record in the approved system and obtain written approval wherever the rule requires it. Keep only the records necessary to show compliance, and share them only with authorized personnel. If the written policy does not address a situation, ask the designated owner rather than extrapolating from an example.

When communicating a decision, distinguish the policy requirement from a manager’s operational choice. Note the policy heading, relevant dates, evidence consulted, approver and any unresolved question. Escalate disputes to the named function where one is specified. The examples in this handbook are educational and do not supersede contracts, applicable law, or approved company policy updates.
