# Records and Data Retention

Fictional Acme Northstar Ltd. internal policy. Version 1.0. Effective 2026-01-01.

## Retention

Routine business correspondence is retained for 3 years unless a legal hold applies.

### Procedure and accountability for retention

Employees should follow the stated retention requirement, retain relevant records when applicable, and contact the responsible department for clarification. Managers must verify requests against the rule stated above. Exceptions require written approval by the policy owner and must not override applicable law. This section is intended to clarify administrative handling of the retention policy and does not create additional entitlements.

## Operational handbook: applied scenarios and decision guidance

The following illustrative procedures explain how to apply the policy rules above. They are fictional examples, not additional entitlements, deadlines or exceptions. Where a scenario is ambiguous, the controlling rule remains the policy statement above and the responsible owner must clarify it.

### Routine correspondence retention

**Rule and interpretation.** Routine business correspondence is retained for three years unless a legal hold applies. Retention periods should not be confused with immediate deletion dates for every record.

**Worked scenario.** An employee cleaning a shared mailbox should check whether messages are routine correspondence and whether a legal hold applies before deleting them.

**Employee workflow.** Identify the record category, consult the applicable retention schedule and check for holds before any authorized disposal.

**Owner responsibilities.** Records owners coordinate classification and approved deletion procedures.

**Scope and limits.** This section does not override legal requirements or authorize ad hoc destruction.

### Executed contract records

**Rule and interpretation.** Executed commercial contracts are retained for seven years after expiration. The start of the seven-year period is contract expiration, not signing.

**Worked scenario.** A contract signed in one year and expiring several years later should be retained for seven years after its actual expiration date.

**Employee workflow.** Record the expiration date, maintain the executed copy in the approved repository and flag amendments that affect the timeline.

**Owner responsibilities.** Legal or records staff confirm disputed dates and the authoritative contract version.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not define retention for unsigned drafts or replace applicable legal holds.

### Controlled deletion

**Rule and interpretation.** Deletion should follow the authorized retention process and must stop where a legal hold applies. An employee should not infer that old files may always be erased.

**Worked scenario.** A team may find a folder older than three years, but some documents may be contracts or subject to a legal hold and therefore require different handling.

**Employee workflow.** Check category, date, legal-hold status and approval before deletion; retain evidence of authorized disposal where the process requires it.

**Owner responsibilities.** Records owners confirm disposition and coordinate with legal counsel for held material.

**Scope and limits.** No particular deletion technology or schedule frequency is imposed here.

### Need-to-know access

**Rule and interpretation.** Records access is restricted to authorized personnel on a need-to-know basis. Employment alone does not create blanket access to every file.

**Worked scenario.** An employee working on procurement may need a specific supplier contract but not unrelated personnel records.

**Employee workflow.** Request access through approved channels; use records only for the authorized purpose; report accidental exposure.

**Owner responsibilities.** System owners review permissions and remove unnecessary access when responsibilities change.

**Scope and limits.** This guidance does not establish a new role hierarchy or universal access matrix.

### Legal holds and release

**Rule and interpretation.** Legal holds suspend routine deletion until legal counsel releases the hold. Only a release from legal counsel ends the suspension under the stated rule.

**Worked scenario.** A project may close while a legal hold remains active; the closure does not authorize deleting the preserved records.

**Employee workflow.** Preserve potentially affected material, avoid routine disposal and ask legal counsel about the scope and release status.

**Owner responsibilities.** Legal counsel controls the release decision; records and IT teams maintain preservation procedures.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not establish a fixed hold duration or authorize managers to lift holds independently.


## Reference workflow and recordkeeping

For any question about this policy, first locate the applicable rule and identify its exact trigger: a calendar-day deadline, a working-day deadline, an approval threshold, a role requirement, or an information-classification restriction. Next, check the relevant record in the approved system and obtain written approval wherever the rule requires it. Keep only the records necessary to show compliance, and share them only with authorized personnel. If the written policy does not address a situation, ask the designated owner rather than extrapolating from an example.

When communicating a decision, distinguish the policy requirement from a manager’s operational choice. Note the policy heading, relevant dates, evidence consulted, approver and any unresolved question. Escalate disputes to the named function where one is specified. The examples in this handbook are educational and do not supersede contracts, applicable law, or approved company policy updates.
