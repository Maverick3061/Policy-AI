## Operational handbook: applied scenarios and decision guidance

The following illustrative procedures explain how to apply the policy rules above. They are fictional examples, not additional entitlements, deadlines or exceptions. Where a scenario is ambiguous, the controlling rule remains the policy statement above and the responsible owner must clarify it.

### Claim timing and receipt quality

**Rule and interpretation.** Reimbursement claims must be submitted within 30 calendar days after the expense and include itemized receipts. A bank-card total alone may not show what was purchased.

**Worked scenario.** For an expense incurred on a business trip, the employee should retain the itemized vendor receipt and submit the claim before the stated calendar-day window ends.

**Employee workflow.** Capture the date, business purpose, currency, amount and itemized receipt; enter the claim in the expense process; respond to finance questions with supporting records.

**Owner responsibilities.** Managers check the business purpose and finance validates supporting documentation.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not establish an automatic grace period or a reimbursement guarantee for late submissions.

### Domestic meal limits

**Rule and interpretation.** The domestic meal reimbursement limit is 45 USD per day. Alcohol is not reimbursable. The daily cap should not be confused with a per-meal allowance.

**Worked scenario.** An employee buying breakfast and dinner on the same domestic travel day should consider their combined eligible meal spending against the 45 USD daily limit.

**Employee workflow.** Keep receipts for meals, identify the relevant travel date and exclude alcohol from the requested reimbursement.

**Owner responsibilities.** Finance applies the published limit and seeks clarification if receipts mix eligible and ineligible items.

**Scope and limits.** This policy does not specify international per-diem amounts, tips or meal allowances for non-travel days.

### Hotel rate approval

**Rule and interpretation.** A hotel stay costing more than 180 USD per night requires preapproval from the finance director. The condition concerns the nightly rate.

**Worked scenario.** A proposed room costing 190 USD per night triggers the preapproval rule even if the employee plans only a single night.

**Employee workflow.** Compare the quoted nightly rate with the threshold before booking; obtain the finance director’s approval where required; retain the written approval with the claim.

**Owner responsibilities.** Finance checks the approval trail against the hotel invoice and business purpose.

**Scope and limits.** The rule does not specify how taxes or fees affect the threshold; seek finance clarification instead of guessing.

### Airfare class and exceptions

**Rule and interpretation.** Economy airfare is the default travel class. Business-class travel requires written executive approval.

**Worked scenario.** A traveler who prefers business class for a long journey must still obtain written executive approval before treating the upgrade as reimbursable.

**Employee workflow.** Record the trip purpose, itinerary and booking choice; attach written approval for a business-class exception; retain fare evidence.

**Owner responsibilities.** The approving executive evaluates the exception; finance verifies the approval before reimbursement.

**Scope and limits.** This guide does not create a distance-based automatic business-class entitlement.

### Review and processing expectations

**Rule and interpretation.** The reimbursement workflow requires clear evidence, timely submission and appropriate approvals. An approved trip does not automatically approve every expense item.

**Worked scenario.** An employee may have an approved hotel booking but still need an itemized receipt and timely claim to complete reimbursement.

**Employee workflow.** Check the claim for missing receipts, explain any unusual item and retain a copy of the submitted record for follow-up.

**Owner responsibilities.** Finance reviews the claim and requests corrections when necessary.

**Scope and limits.** No payment date, exchange-rate method or dispute deadline is added by this explanatory section.
